Edge Industries

Operations Lead · Kota Zone · Jun 2025 – Jan 2026

The situation

Edge Industries was scaling a utility asset digitization program across multiple cities. Teams were deployed against incomplete, inaccurate data. Nobody knew the real asset count on the ground. Previous benchmark: 2 cities in 2 years. I was given 5 people and one city, starting from zero.

What was broken

  • No ground-truth data — asset lists sourced from incomplete internal records, teams deployed blind
  • Sequential deployment — one team, one starting point, dead travel time between assets
  • No verification structure — quality unchecked, errors caught late or not at all
  • No onboarding system — every new hire learned verbally, inconsistent output from day one

What I built

  • Ground-truth database — sourced real asset data directly from government planning departments, replacing internal records
  • Convergence deployment model — split each team into Team A and Team B, assigned opposite ends of each zone, both converging to the middle simultaneously
  • Deployment calendars — planned around geography, weather patterns, and local holiday schedules
  • 3-layer verification chain — field associate → operations manager (daily) → computer operator (weekly) → me
  • Dedicated substation team — 6 people across 3 cities assigned solely to substation digitization
  • DISCOM relationships — direct contacts with state electricity board employees for ground intelligence
  • Role-specific SOPs — structured onboarding for every position, replacing verbal handoffs

Neelkanth Engineering

Operations & Delivery Lead · Electrical Panels · Mar 2023 – Feb 2025

The situation

Neelkanth manufactured electrical distribution panels and circuit boxes. Orders were inconsistent — 4 clients per month. Three separate facilities bleeding rent. Deliveries partial and late. No scheduling system, no pipeline, no consolidated operations.

What was broken

  • No delivery scheduling — partial shipments and missed timelines damaging client trust
  • Wrong location — warehouse 45 min to 1.5 hrs from main highway, unpredictable transport
  • 3 separate facilities — manufacturing, warehouse, office each paying rent, fragmented operations
  • Thin pipeline — 4 clients per month with no system to grow or retain

What I built

  • Projected delivery timeline system — scheduled all orders by capacity, renegotiated delivery windows upfront, eliminated partial shipments
  • Strategic warehouse relocation — proposed and executed move near Dwarka–Gurgaon Expressway, cutting transport unpredictability
  • Facility consolidation — merged 3 locations into 2, office inside warehouse, professional rebrand
  • End-to-end delivery ownership — contract to final payment, client representative QA inspections before every shipment
  • Client pipeline — grew from reactive order-taking to 20 contracted clients with forward commitments

Avant Garde

Founder's Office · Jewellery D2C · Mar 2022 – Feb 2023

The situation

Avant Garde made resin and clay jewellery for teenagers and young couples. Selling 10–15 units per week through physical college fest stalls. No online presence, no demand validation, no distribution system. Products made on instinct.

What was broken

  • No feedback loop — products manufactured on guesswork, no validation before production spend
  • Single channel dependency — 100% revenue from physical college fest stalls, no digital fallback
  • No distribution leverage — growing alone, no partnerships, no network effect

What I built

  • Community design loop — collected design ideas via Instagram and Facebook, let audience vote, only winning designs went to production
  • Brand collaboration network — partnered with complementary small brands, cross-promoted, built distribution at zero ad spend
  • College fest stall network — systematised the physical sales channel across multiple fests as primary revenue engine
